PROPOSED CLIENT STANDARD

Shared gameplay.
Personal style.

Standardize the parts of the client that affect a match. Give players simple ways to customize everything within that boundary.

The minimum we’d standardize

Arena owners choose the rules. Clients should interpret those rules consistently, report player actions faithfully, and preserve the same information and timing that players rely on in a match.

  • Encode and interpret gameplay messages consistently, including movement, weapons, and arena settings.
  • Follow the same timing and handling for input, shots, and server corrections.
  • Keep visibility, gameplay cues, and the actions available to players consistent.

Make it yours with themes

Start with built-in color controls and shareable theme packs. A player could publish a palette for menus and other interface elements, and another player could preview, install, or remove it from the approved client.

Theme packs could be data files with a limited set of fields. Preserve the visibility and meaning of gameplay cues, including team colors and indicators used during play.

How a theme could work

Choose an interface palette, preview it, then export it for a friend to import. The same client build and arena rules stay in effect.

Test changes that affect play

Use a test arena and repeat the same scenarios before rolling gameplay changes into the shared build. Publish the client revision and the results so players can compare them.

Two updates in one frame

Receive a shot and a movement update in both orders. The shot appears once and the ship ends at its newest position.

Late weapon report

Deliver an older shot after a newer position. The shot uses its reported origin and the ship keeps its newer position.

Turning under jitter

Compare both builds on the same arena and replay. Measure visible corrections, missed shots, and packet rate.
